## What It Costs to Buy in Greece (/costs)

A clear breakdown of what each professional and tax typically costs in 2026, so you can budget with confidence.

These are typical 2026 market ranges for a residential purchase. Real figures depend on the property, the region and the work involved — every professional on Vilaow gives you an exact written quote before you commit. Prices marked + VAT carry Greek VAT of 24% on top.

### Professional Fees

- **Property lawyer**: 1%–2% + VAT. Title & ownership checks, due diligence, contracts, AFM tax number, power of attorney. On a €300k purchase, roughly €3,000–€6,000.
- **Notary**: 0.8%–1.5% + VAT. Mandatory. Drafts and certifies the purchase deed and registers the transaction.
- **Real estate agent**: 2%–2.5% + VAT. Usually paid by the buyer. Finds, shows and negotiates the property.
- **Civil engineer — inspection**: from ~€400. Technical/legal check of the property against its permits. A basic legal inspection can start near €150; a full independent building survey starts from ~€5,000.
- **Architect**: ~4% of build cost. For renovation or new build — design, drawings and permit submissions. Architecture + engineering combined often runs ~7% of the build budget.
- **Contractor (building)**: €1,700–€2,400 / m². Traditional new build. Simpler builds can start near €800–€1,300/m²; premium specs run higher.
- **Tax advisor / accountant**: varies. AFM setup, annual ENFIA property tax, rental-income filings and ongoing tax matters.
- **Translation / interpreter**: €150–€400. Document translation for foreign buyers; more for complex or certified work.

### Taxes & Official Fees

- **Property transfer tax**: ~3.09%. Of the taxable or commercial value (whichever is higher). Paid to the state before the deed is signed. The single biggest cost.
- **Land Registry / Cadastre**: ~0.5%–0.7%. Registers the new ownership in your name.
- **Municipal surcharge**: ~3% of the tax. A small surcharge added on top of the transfer tax.
- **New-build VAT**: 24%. Applies only when a brand-new home is sold by a construction company (transfer tax does not apply in that case).

### The Complete Buying Process in Greece, Step by Step (2026)

Category: Buying Process. Read it at /guides?guide=the-complete-buying-process-in-greece-step-by-step-2026

From AFM number to keys in hand — the stages of a Greek purchase and how long each takes.

#### Before You Start: Two Things You Cannot Skip

Every foreign buyer in Greece needs an AFM (Greek tax number) and a clear paper trail for funds arriving from abroad. The AFM takes a day or two to obtain through a lawyer with power of attorney. Nothing else can proceed without it.

#### Stage 1 — Find the Property and Agree Terms

Offers in Greece are typically verbal at first. Once agreed, a reservation deposit (often €3,000–10,000) may be paid to take the property off the market. Make sure any deposit is refundable if legal checks fail — this clause matters more than almost anything else you sign.

#### Stage 2 — Legal Due Diligence

Your lawyer checks the title at the Land Registry and Cadastre, confirms the seller owns what they claim to sell, and searches for mortgages, liens, claims and unpaid taxes attached to the property. In parallel, an engineer confirms the building matches its permits — unpermitted additions are extremely common and become your problem after signing.

#### Stage 3 — The Pre-Contract (Optional but Common)

For purchases with a delay before completion — off-plan, finance, or paperwork fixes — a notarised pre-contract locks both sides in, usually with a 10% deposit. If the seller withdraws, they typically owe the deposit back doubled.

#### Stage 4 — Tax Payment and the Final Deed

Before signing, the buyer pays the transfer tax. The final contract is signed before a notary — a public official who is mandatory in Greece — usually with both lawyers present.

#### Stage 5 — Registration

The deed must be registered at the Land Registry/Cadastre. Until registration, your ownership is not fully protected against third parties. Ask for the registration certificate as your final proof.

Note: Typical timeline: 6–10 weeks for a clean resale property, longer if permits need fixing or the property has inheritance complications.

### Golden Visa 2026: The New Thresholds, Explained Simply

Category: Golden Visa. Read it at /guides?guide=golden-visa-2026-the-new-thresholds-explained-simply

€800k, €400k or €250k? Where each tier applies, the 120 m² rule, and the rental restrictions.

#### The Three Tiers

Since the 2024 reform, the minimum investment depends on where you buy: €800,000 in Attica (greater Athens), Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and islands with more than 3,100 inhabitants; €400,000 in the rest of the country; and a special €250,000 tier for two cases — converting a commercial building to residential use, or restoring a listed building.

#### The 120 m² Rule

For the €800k and €400k tiers, the investment must be a single property of at least 120 m². Combining several small apartments no longer works in these tiers. The €250k conversion/restoration route has no minimum size.

#### The Short-Term Rental Ban

Properties bought under the new rules cannot be let on short-term rental platforms. Violations carry heavy fines and can affect the permit. Long-term rental remains allowed — factor this into your yield expectations.

#### What the Visa Gives You

A five-year renewable residence permit for you, your spouse, children under 21 and both sets of parents. No minimum stay requirement — you keep the permit as long as you keep the property. It is residence, not citizenship.

Note: Golden Visa rules have changed twice in recent years and may change again. A vetted lawyer will verify the rules that apply on your signing date.

### Transfer Tax, ENFIA and Every Tax You’ll Pay

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=transfer-tax-enfia-and-every-tax-you-will-actually-pay

The real Greek tax picture — at purchase, every year after, and when you sell.

#### At Purchase: Transfer Tax

Resale properties carry a transfer tax of 3.09% of the taxable value. New builds sold by a developer with a post-2006 permit are in principle subject to 24% VAT — but a VAT suspension has applied in recent years, meaning most new-build buyers also pay 3.09%. Confirm which regime applies to your property before budgeting.

#### Every Year: ENFIA

ENFIA is the annual ownership tax, calculated from size, age, zone value and features. A typical holiday home commonly lands in the hundreds of euros per year; luxury properties in high-zone-value areas pay substantially more. Get an estimate before you buy, not after.

#### If You Rent It Out

Rental income for individuals is taxed on a progressive scale: 15% on the first €12,000, 35% from €12,001 to €35,000, and 45% above that.

#### When You Sell

A capital gains tax for individual sellers exists in law but has been repeatedly suspended — under suspension, individuals generally do not pay it. Verify the status in your sale year.

#### Do Not Forget

- Notary: roughly 0.8–1.2% plus VAT
- Legal fees: typically 1–1.5% plus VAT
- Agent commission: commonly ~2% plus VAT
- Registration: ~0.5%

Note: Rule of thumb: budget roughly 8–10% on top of the price to cover all taxes and fees on a resale.

### Due Diligence: The Checks That Protect Your Purchase

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=due-diligence-the-checks-that-save-buyers-tens-of-thousands

Debts, permits, land rules — what a proper legal and technical check covers.

#### Why Greece Is Different

Greece completed its national Cadastre only recently, and decades of informal building mean paperwork problems are the rule, not the exception. The good news: nearly every trap is detectable in advance. The bad news: skipping the checks transfers the problem to you, permanently.

#### The Legal Check

- Title chain: tracing ownership back at least 20 years to confirm the seller really owns the property and no heir has a claim.
- Burdens: mortgages, prenotations, liens and seizures registered against the property — these transfer with it.
- Tax clearance: the seller must produce certificates showing no unpaid property taxes; without them the notary cannot sign.

#### The Technical Check

- Permit match: the engineer compares the actual building to its permit. Closed balconies, extra rooms, converted basements — all common, all must be legalised before transfer.
- Boundary check: the topographic survey is compared with the Cadastre. Discrepancies of metres are common in rural areas.
- Land status: outside town plans, the plot must be verified as buildable and not classified as forest or archaeological land.

#### What It Costs vs What It Saves

Full legal and technical due diligence typically costs €1,500–3,000 combined. Done properly, these checks find problems while they’re still easy to fix: before you sign. It is the cheapest insurance in the entire transaction.

### Athens vs Crete vs Thessaloniki: Choosing Where to Buy

Yields, prices, lifestyle and liquidity — an honest comparison of the three main markets.

#### Athens — Liquidity and Yield

The capital offers the deepest rental market, year-round demand, and the easiest resale. Prices have risen strongly since 2017 but remain below most Western European capitals. Best for investors who prioritise income and exit options. Note: Athens sits in the €800k Golden Visa tier.

#### Crete — The Island That Works All Year

The largest Greek island has real cities, an economy beyond tourism, two international airports, and a long season. Holiday lets perform strongly April–October, and the €400k Golden Visa tier applies across most of the island. A strong choice for buyers who want a home first and an asset second.

The second city offers the lowest entry prices of the three, a large student and professional rental base, and an improving infrastructure story. The trade-off: a less international resale market.

- Strongest lifestyle value: Crete
- Easiest international resale: Athens, then Crete
- Lowest entry price: Thessaloniki

Note: The biggest mistake we see: choosing a region from photos. Spend a week there in the off-season — February tells you more than July ever will.

### Renovating in Greece: Permits, Costs and Managing a Build Remotely

Category: Renovation & Build. Read it at /guides?guide=renovating-in-greece-permits-costs-and-managing-a-build-remotely

What renovation really costs per square metre, which works need permits, and how to manage from abroad.

#### What Needs a Permit

Greece distinguishes between a full building permit (structural changes, extensions, change of use) and the simpler small-scale works approval (most internal renovations, energy upgrades, roof repairs). Purely cosmetic work generally needs no permit. Your engineer determines the category — never let a contractor decide it informally.

#### Realistic Costs per Square Metre

- Cosmetic refresh: €250–450/m²
- Full renovation (electrics, plumbing, bathrooms, kitchen): €700–1,100/m²
- Heavy / structural: €1,100–1,600/m²
- New build: €1,500–2,200/m²

Island projects run 10–25% above mainland prices. Ask for itemised quotes; they make it easy to compare builders and track the work.

#### The Contract Is Your Protection

Insist on a written contract with milestone payments tied to completed, inspected stages — never large sums up front. Include a bill of quantities, brand specifications, a timeline with delay penalties, and a retention amount (5–10%) held until snagging is complete.

#### Managing from Abroad

The projects that go wrong are almost always the unsupervised ones. Appoint a supervising engineer independent of the contractor, with weekly photo reports and stage sign-offs before each payment. The cost — typically 5–8% of the works — pays for itself in prevented shortcuts.

### Your AFM and Greek Bank Account: The Unglamorous First Step

Category: Practical. Read it at /guides?guide=your-afm-and-greek-bank-account-the-unglamorous-first-step

How to get your Greek tax number fast, whether you need a local account, and how to move money correctly.

#### The AFM — Your Greek Tax Number

Nothing happens in Greece without an AFM: you cannot buy, open utilities, or pay taxes. The fast route is giving your lawyer power of attorney — usually done within a day or two. In person, you visit the tax office for non-residents with your passport.

#### Do You Need a Greek Bank Account?

A purchase can complete with funds sent from abroad, but a Greek account makes everything easier: utilities, ENFIA, contractors, management. Opening one as a non-resident requires patience: passport, AFM, proof of address, source of funds. Some banks are friendlier to non-residents — ask your lawyer which branch to use.

#### Moving the Money — The Part That Protects You

Always transfer the purchase money bank to bank with clear references. The paper trail proves source of funds and creates the import record that matters if you later sell and repatriate proceeds. Never agree to pay any part in cash — an under-declared price reduces your protection and raises your tax exposure later.

Note: Start the AFM and banking process the week you decide to buy — not the week you find the house.

### The Notary: Why Every Greek Purchase Runs Through One

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=the-notary-why-every-greek-purchase-runs-through-one

What the notary does, what they do not do, and why you still need your own lawyer.

#### A Public Official, Not Your Advisor

In Greece, the transfer deed must be executed before a notary — a state-appointed official who drafts the contract, verifies documents, confirms taxes are paid, and gives the deed public force. No notary, no transfer. Their fee is roughly 0.8–1.2% of the contract value.

#### What the Notary Checks

- Identity and capacity of both parties
- The title documents and certificates presented
- That transfer tax has been paid
- That mandatory certificates (energy, engineer compliance, tax clearance) are attached

#### What the Notary Does NOT Do

The notary is neutral. They do not investigate whether the price is fair, whether the building matches its permit on the ground, or whether buying this property is wise. They check papers, not reality. This is exactly why your own lawyer and engineer are non-negotiable — they are the only people in the room working for you.

Note: The seller often proposes their preferred notary. That is normal and usually fine — your protection comes from your own lawyer, before you ever reach the notary.

### 8 Common Buying Mistakes, and How to Avoid Each One

Category: Buying Process. Read it at /guides?guide=the-traps-foreign-buyers-fall-into-and-how-each-is-avoided

Eight things we see go wrong, and the simple check that prevents each one.

#### 1. Choose a Lawyer Who Works Only for You

The agent offers to arrange everything, including a lawyer they know. That lawyer’s next ten deals depend on the agent — not on you. Always appoint a lawyer with no connection to the selling side.

#### 2. Match the Size to the Permit

The listing says 120 m²; the permit says 80. Sometimes the extra can be legalised for a fee; sometimes it cannot. Either way the price should reflect it — and only an engineer’s check reveals it.

#### 3. Make Your Deposit Refundable

Put any deposit in writing, refundable if the checks find a problem. Then a surprise costs you nothing.

#### 4. Confirm Every Owner Agrees to Sell

The seller is one of four heirs; the others have not agreed to sell. The title check catches this — handshakes do not.

#### 5. Check the Land Is Buildable

A beautiful plot that can never be built on. Land outside town plans must be verified as buildable before any money moves.

#### 6. Declare the Full Price

An under-declared price saves the seller tax and exposes you: lower protection, higher capital gain later, and an illegal act. Decline firmly.

#### 7. Add Your Own Terms to the Contract

Off-plan contracts drafted by the developer protect the developer. Yours must add milestone payments, delay penalties, and a completion guarantee.

#### 8. Register Your Ownership

Until the deed is registered at the Cadastre, your ownership is incomplete. Always obtain the registration certificate.

### Renting Out Your Greek Property: Long-Term vs Short-Term Rules

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=renting-out-your-greek-property-long-term-vs-short-term-rules

Registration numbers, short-term restrictions, taxes on rent, and what actually yields more.

#### Short-Term (Airbnb-Style)

Every short-term rental must be registered and display its registration number (AMA) on every listing. Recent rule-tightening added liability insurance, safety equipment and quality standards — and gave saturated central Athens districts the power to freeze new registrations. If short-term income is central to your plan, verify the current rules for your municipality before buying.

#### The Golden Visa Exception

Properties acquired under the post-2024 Golden Visa rules are barred from short-term rental entirely. Long-term tenancies remain allowed.

#### Long-Term Tenancies

Standard residential leases run a minimum of three years by law, even if the contract says less. Deposits of one to two months are customary. Eviction for non-payment works but takes months — tenant selection matters more than the contract.

#### The Tax on Your Rent

Individual landlords pay 15% on the first €12,000 of annual rent, 35% to €35,000, and 45% above. All leases must be declared electronically — undeclared rent is easily detected through platform data sharing.

#### Which Yields More?

In tourist zones, well-run short-term lets can gross 1.5–2.5× long-term rent — before management (20–30%), platform fees, cleaning and vacancy. After costs the gap narrows substantially. In cities, long-term often wins on a net, headache-adjusted basis.

### Buying Off-Plan or New-Build with Confidence

Category: Renovation & Build. Read it at /guides?guide=buying-off-plan-or-new-build-how-to-do-it-without-losing-sleep

Stage payments, completion guarantees, snagging and the developer checks that prevent disputes.

#### Check the Developer Before the Property

Visit their completed projects and talk to owners who bought from them. Verify that the building permit for your project is issued and final — renderings are not permits. A lawyer can confirm the plot’s title and permit status in days.

#### The Payment Schedule Is Everything

Pay as each construction stage is finished and certified by your own engineer: foundation, frame, roof, closures, finishes. A safe pattern: 10–15% at contract, stage payments through construction, and 10%+ held until delivery and snagging.

#### Lock the Specification

The contract must include the full technical description: materials, brands or equivalent standards, energy class, and what exactly is included. Vague specs become disputes at delivery.

#### Delay and Exit Clauses

Include a binding completion date with penalties for delay, and a right to withdraw with your money back if delay exceeds an agreed limit. Greek courts enforce clear written penalties; they cannot enforce optimism.

#### Delivery and Snagging

Before final payment, walk the property with your engineer against the contract spec, list every defect in writing, and agree a deadline for fixes.

### Mainland vs Island: The Practical Differences to Know

Category: Regions. Read it at /guides?guide=mainland-vs-island-the-practical-differences-nobody-tells-you

Ferries, winter closures, build costs, healthcare and resale — the realities of island ownership.

#### Access Shapes Everything

An island that takes one flight is a different asset from one that takes a flight, a ferry and a taxi. Access determines your rental season, renovation costs, maintenance response time, and resale audience. Count the steps from your home airport to the front door — in February, not August.

#### The Winter Question

Many smaller islands effectively close from November to March: restaurants shut, ferries thin out, trades become scarce. Crete, Rhodes, Corfu and the larger islands keep functioning year-round. If you plan winter visits or year-round letting, this single factor should drive the shortlist.

#### Costs Run Higher on Islands

- Construction and renovation: typically +10–25% vs mainland
- Maintenance: fewer trades, longer waits, travel charges
- Insurance and logistics: marginally higher across the board

#### Healthcare and Practicalities

Larger islands have hospitals; small ones have clinics and a helicopter for emergencies. For older buyers this is not a detail.

#### Resale Liquidity

Mainland cities and the famous islands resell fastest internationally. A beautiful house on a little-known island can take years to sell — wonderful to own, hard to exit. Buy the lifestyle you want, but know which kind of asset you are buying.

Note: Our rule: if you would not happily spend a random Tuesday in November there, think twice before buying there.

### Can Foreigners Buy Property in Greece? Yes — Here’s How

Category: Golden Visa. Read it at /guides?guide=can-foreigners-buy-property-in-greece-yes-here-is-how

EU and non-EU buyers, the border-area permit, and the simple path to owning Greek property.

#### The Short Answer

Yes. Greece places very few restrictions on foreign property ownership. EU citizens are treated essentially like Greeks. Non-EU citizens can also buy freely across almost the entire country.

#### The One Exception: Border Areas

In designated border regions and some islands, non-EU buyers need a permit — a straightforward administrative application your lawyer handles. It is rarely refused for residential purchases, but it must be done before completion.

#### What Every Foreign Buyer Needs

- An AFM (Greek tax number)
- A clear, documented source of funds
- A lawyer and, in practice, a notary

#### Ownership Is Full and Permanent

Foreign buyers get the same freehold ownership as locals — you can live in it, rent it, renovate it, leave it to heirs, or sell it. Owning property does not by itself grant residency, but qualifying investments can open the Golden Visa route.

Note: Buying is open to almost everyone. The work is in the due diligence, not in eligibility.

### Understanding Building Rights in Greece: ΣΔ, Coverage and Setbacks

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=understanding-building-rights-in-greece-coverage-and-setbacks

How much you can actually build on a Greek plot — the building coefficient, coverage ratio and the rules that decide it.

#### The Building Coefficient (ΣΔ)

The συντελεστής δόμησης (building coefficient) is the single most important number for any plot. It sets the total floor area you may build relative to plot size. A 1,000 m² plot with a coefficient of 0.4 allows 400 m² of construction. Inside town plans it is published per zone; outside, special rules apply.

#### Coverage Ratio (κάλυψη)

Separately, the coverage ratio limits how much of the plot the building footprint may occupy — often 40–60%. Even with floor area to spare, you cannot exceed the footprint, which pushes designs upward rather than outward.

#### Setbacks and Height

Minimum distances from boundaries and maximum building heights further shape what is possible. These vary by zone and must be checked against the specific plot, not assumed.

#### Outside the Town Plan

On rural land, the key threshold is whether the plot is artiotita (buildable) — usually requiring a minimum size (often 4,000 m²) and road frontage, with exceptions for older plots. Forest and archaeological classifications can override everything.

Note: Never buy land for its size alone. An engineer reading the coefficient, coverage and classification tells you what you can truly build.

### Power of Attorney in Greece: Buying Without Flying Back and Forth

Category: Practical. Read it at /guides?guide=power-of-attorney-in-greece-buying-without-flying-back-and-forth

How a POA lets your lawyer act for you, what it should and should not allow, and how to set it up safely.

#### Why It Matters

A Greek purchase has many steps that legally require your presence or signature. A power of attorney (POA) lets your trusted lawyer handle them — obtaining your AFM, signing documents, even completing at the notary — so you do not need to fly to Greece for every stage.

#### What a Good POA Covers

- Obtaining your tax number and opening accounts
- Conducting due diligence and submitting filings
- Signing the deed, where you authorise it

#### Keeping It Safe

A POA is powerful, so scope it carefully. Limit it to the specific transaction, set an expiry, and consider requiring your explicit confirmation before the final signing. Use a lawyer you have vetted independently — never one introduced solely by the seller or agent.

#### How It Is Made

A POA for Greek property is signed before a notary, either in Greece or at a Greek consulate abroad, or made locally and apostilled and translated. Your lawyer will tell you the cleanest route for your country.

Note: A scoped, time-limited POA to an independent lawyer is safe and normal. A broad, open-ended one to the seller’s contact is not.

### Objective Value vs Market Value: The Tax Number That Matters

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=objective-value-vs-market-value-the-tax-number-that-matters

Why Greek property tax is based on a government value, not your price — and how it affects your costs.

#### Two Different Numbers

Every Greek property has an objective value (αντικειμενική αξία) set by the state from location, size and features — and a market value, the price actually paid. They are often different.

#### Why It Matters

Transfer tax and many fees are calculated on the higher of the objective value or the declared price. ENFIA annual tax is based on the objective value. So two homes selling for the same price can carry different taxes if their objective values differ.

#### Why the Declared Price Must Be the Real Price

Some sellers suggest declaring a price near the objective value to cut tax. Beyond being illegal, it leaves you exposed: a lower declared cost base increases your taxable gain if capital gains tax is reinstated when you sell, and it weakens your legal position. Always declare the true price.

#### Use It in Your Budget

Ask your lawyer for the objective value early — it tells you the tax base before you commit, and occasionally reveals that a property is priced below its official value, which has its own implications.

Note: Know the objective value before you sign. It drives your tax, not the sticker price.

### ENFIA Explained: The Annual Property Tax in Greece

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=enfia-explained-the-annual-property-tax-in-greece

How the yearly ownership tax is calculated, what to expect, and how to estimate it before you buy.

#### What ENFIA Is

ENFIA is Greece’s annual property ownership tax, paid by whoever owns the property on 1 January each year. It has a main tax based on the property and, for higher-value portfolios, a supplementary tax.

#### How It Is Calculated

The main tax derives from the objective value drivers: location zone, size, age, floor, frontage and use. A typical holiday apartment often falls in the low hundreds of euros per year, while large or high-zone-value properties pay considerably more.

#### Estimate Before You Buy

Ask the seller for their recent ENFIA assessment or your accountant for an estimate from the property’s details. It is a recurring cost that should sit in your budget from day one, not a surprise the following spring.

#### Paying It

ENFIA is issued through the tax platform and can be paid in instalments across the year. Your Greek accountant typically handles it alongside your annual filing.

Note: Get the ENFIA figure during due diligence — it is small but permanent, and easy to find out in advance.

### Reservation Agreements and Deposits: Reserve with Confidence

Category: Buying Process. Read it at /guides?guide=reservation-agreements-and-deposits-protecting-your-money

How to reserve a property and get your deposit back if the checks don’t go to plan.

#### What a Reservation Does

Once you agree a price, a reservation agreement with a deposit signals commitment and takes the property off the market while due diligence runs. Deposits commonly range from €3,000 to €10,000, or a small percentage.

#### The Clause That Protects You

The single most important term: your deposit is fully refundable if legal or technical due diligence uncovers a material problem — bad title, unpermitted construction, undisclosed burdens. With this clause, you get your deposit back if a check fails.

#### Reservation vs Pre-Contract

A reservation is light and short-term. A notarised pre-contract is heavier, used when there is a real delay to completion, and typically carries a 10% deposit with the double-back protection if the seller withdraws.

#### Never Pay Informally

Pay any deposit by traceable bank transfer against a written agreement your lawyer has reviewed — never cash on a handshake or a one-line receipt.

Note: A deposit is fine — but only with a written refund-on-failed-checks clause and a bank paper trail.

### Inheritance and Greek Property: What Foreign Owners Should Plan For

Category: Legal & Tax. Read it at /guides?guide=inheritance-and-greek-property-what-foreign-owners-should-plan-for

How Greek property passes to heirs, the tax involved, and why a little planning saves a lot.

#### Greek Property, Greek Rules

Real estate located in Greece is generally subject to Greek inheritance procedures, regardless of where you live. Your heirs will deal with the Greek system to transfer the property.

#### Inheritance Tax

Greek inheritance tax depends on the relationship and value, with generous allowances for close family (spouses and children) and higher rates for distant relations. Close-family transfers of typical homes often face modest tax thanks to the allowances.

#### The EU Succession Rules

EU regulations let many foreign owners elect the law of their nationality to govern succession, which can simplify matters — but the Greek tax and transfer process still applies to the asset. Cross-border estates get complex quickly.

#### Plan Ahead

A short conversation with a lawyer experienced in cross-border estates — and a properly drafted will covering the Greek asset — spares your heirs months of difficulty and avoids costly mistakes.

Note: This is general information, not legal advice. Cross-border inheritance varies by country — get tailored advice for your situation.

Moving large sums internationally, proving source of funds, and keeping completion on schedule.

### Healthcare and Residency in Greece for Foreign Property Owners

Category: Practical. Read it at /guides?guide=healthcare-and-residency-in-greece-for-foreign-property-owners

Access to healthcare, the residency question, and what owning a home does and does not give you.

#### Owning Is Not Residing

Buying property does not automatically grant residency. EU citizens can live in Greece freely and register after staying; non-EU citizens need a visa or permit — which is where the Golden Visa investment route comes in.

#### Healthcare Access

Greece has a public health system and a strong private sector. EU citizens can use the European health card for visits and, once resident and contributing, access the public system. Non-EU owners typically rely on private health insurance until they hold residency.

#### The Golden Visa and Health

Golden Visa holders generally need private health insurance as part of their status, rather than automatic public cover. Private cover in Greece is relatively affordable and widely used by international owners.

#### Practical Planning

Larger cities and islands have hospitals and English-speaking private clinics; remote areas have clinics and emergency transfer. For older buyers, proximity to a hospital should weigh in the location choice.

Note: This is general guidance — residency and health rules depend on nationality and status. Confirm your specific case with a lawyer.

What daily life really costs — from groceries and utilities to dining and transport, by region.
